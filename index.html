Projects

  • Corrosion Inspection

    AI that finds and grades rust on steel (Personal project)
    • Built a deep learning model (DeepLabV3+ with a ResNet-50 backbone) that labels every pixel of a steel photo as fair, poor or severe corrosion, trained on 440 expert-labelled bridge images.
    • Reached a weighted F1 of 0.875 on unseen images, in line with the published 0.867, using class weights and Dice loss to handle rare severe corrosion.
    • Turned predictions into ranked repair zones and coating-cost savings, exported the model to ONNX (under 1 second per image on a CPU) and deployed a live web app.

  • Burnout in Tech Workers

    Predicting burnout from work and lifestyle habits (Master's project)
    • Analysed 100,000 tech workers and found burnout rises with work hours (r = 0.47) and meetings per day (r = 0.46), and falls with sleep (r = −0.34) and vacation days taken (r = −0.28).
    • Built a linear regression model after removing 12 columns that would leak the answer (such as stress score). It explained 71% of the variation in burnout (R² = 0.71) on 20,000 unseen employees, with an average error of 1.15 points versus 2.2 for a baseline.
    • Training and test scores matched (R² 0.71 on both), so the model holds up on new people. The strongest drivers were work hours (+1.04), sleep (−0.90) and meetings (+0.89).

  • Student Performance Analysis

    Do students do better in Portuguese or Maths, and in the city or the countryside? (Master's group project)
    • Analysed final grades of 382 Portuguese secondary school students who took both Portuguese and Maths (UCI Student Performance data).
    • A paired t-test showed students score higher in Portuguese than in Maths (t = 9.98, p < 0.001), by 1.7 to 2.5 points out of 20 (95% confidence interval, confirmed by bootstrap).
    • Two-sample t-tests showed urban students outperform rural students in both subjects: Portuguese (t = 3.17, p = 0.001) and Maths (t = 1.83, p = 0.034).
    • Checked every test's conditions with Q-Q plots before trusting the results, and set out what the data can and cannot show.

  • Smart Crop Advisor

    AI that recommends what to grow (Bachelor's team project)
    • Built a working IoT crop advisor: soil moisture, pH and temperature-humidity (DHT11) sensors feed live field readings to an Arduino, which shows them on an LCD and drives a relay-controlled water pump and buzzer alert.
    • Trained and compared machine learning models on soil, weather and sensor data. Random Forest reached 99.8% accuracy, 99.8% precision and 99.8% recall, ahead of KNN at 97.5% accuracy.
    • Served the model through a Flask web app: entering soil moisture, temperature, humidity, pH, rainfall and location returns the best crop to plant.
